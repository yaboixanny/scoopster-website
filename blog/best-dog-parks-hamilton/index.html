Best Dog Parks in Hamilton: 2026 Guide

Published December 20, 2025 · Updated October 24, 2026 • By Scoopster • Local Expert Guide

Looking for dog parks in Hamilton where your dog can run off leash, or a trail for a slower walk together? Start by checking the boundary: a designated free-run area, a fenced dog park and a conservation trail offer different outings.

This 2026 guide compares Ancaster’s Corporal Nathan Cirillo Free Run Area, Borer’s Falls in Dundas and Heritage Green in Stoney Creek, followed by two nearby leashed walks. The choices below are grouped by the kind of visit you want, rather than a claim that one park suits every dog.

Checked October 24, 2026: locations and designations were reviewed against City of Hamilton and conservation authority information. Check the official map and posted signs before each visit; gates, access and conditions can change.

Ancaster Dog Park: Corporal Nathan Cirillo Free Run Area

Find it: 799 Golf Links Road, beside the cinema site. The City also identifies it by its former name, Cinema Park.

If you’re searching for an Ancaster dog park near the Meadowlands, this is the designated free-run option to look up first. The City’s published map classifies it as unfenced. Some third-party listings describe fencing differently; don’t plan a visit around an assumed secure perimeter. Check the current entrance signs and boundaries yourself.

Decide whether free running fits your dog

Choose an off-leash outing only if you can keep your dog under control. For a dog that follows scents, bolts toward other dogs or is still learning recall, a leashed walk may be a better plan. Walk the boundary before releasing the leash, and keep the leash ready for the return to your vehicle.

Bring water, waste bags and a towel for the trip home. Don’t rely on an unverified stream, water station or separate small-dog enclosure being available. If a fenced small-dog space is the priority, compare the Dundas option below rather than treating all Hamilton dog parks as interchangeable.

Prefer a shared walk to free running? See the Ancaster and Dundas trail alternatives below. For help keeping your own lawn usable between outings, check our Ancaster yard-cleanup coverage.

Dundas Dog Park: Borer’s Falls Leash-Free Area

Find it: 491 York Road, at Valley Road. The City’s map lists Borer’s Falls as fenced and marks a small-dog area.

For owners comparing a Dundas dog park with the Ancaster free-run area, the enclosure is the useful distinction. Start here if you want to investigate a fenced setting or a separate space for a smaller dog. Inspect gates and fencing on arrival, and check which enclosure the entrance signs identify for your dog.

A small-dog area does not guarantee a quiet visit

Watch the dogs already inside before entering. If yours looks overwhelmed or play is becoming rough, wait or choose a walk instead. Crowd levels and the mix of dogs can change from one visit to the next, so have a leashed-walk alternative in mind.

Use the York Road address when navigating. The dog park is not the waterfall trail: a search for Borer’s Falls can also lead you toward the conservation area and hiking access. An off-leash designation in the dog park does not extend to surrounding trails or the trip from your car.

If your dog would rather sniff beside you than mingle, Dundas Valley’s leashed trails offer another kind of outing. Back at home, our Dundas dog poop pickup service can help with the yard routine.

Stoney Creek Option: Heritage Green Community Dog Park

Heritage Green is another fenced choice on the City’s dog-park map. Search for Heritage Green Community Trust Leash-Free Dog Park in the live finder before travelling: City documents use different street numbers on First Road West, so use the mapped park entrance rather than a guessed address.

Consider it when an east-side outing makes more sense than driving across Hamilton to Ancaster or Dundas. As at any shared enclosure, look at the activity inside first, keep entrances clear and leave if your dog is uncomfortable. A fence is a useful boundary, not a substitute for supervision.

Two Leashed Walks Near Ancaster and Dundas

A dog-friendly park is not necessarily an off-leash park. For a dog that prefers walking beside you to group play, try one of these nearby routes.

Dundas Valley Conservation Area

The main entrance is at 650 Governors Road. Ancaster-side access includes the Hermitage lot at 621 Sulphur Springs Road and the Merrick lot at 380 Lower Lions Club Road. The network includes the Main Loop, Bruce Trail and Hamilton to Brantford Rail Trail.

Dogs must remain leashed throughout the conservation area. Choose a route for your own mobility and your dog’s stamina; HCA notes that the rugged trails have limited accessibility and points wheelchair and scooter users toward the Rail Trail. Check the official Dundas Valley page for the map, admission and current closures. Pack waste out where bins aren’t available.

Tiffany Falls Conservation Area

At 900 Wilson Street East, Ancaster, Tiffany Falls is a waterfall outing rather than a place to let your dog run. Keep your dog leashed and stay on the designated gravel path and viewing platform; HCA prohibits access to the bottom of the falls.

The small site has limited parking and no garbage receptacles, so take bagged waste home with you. Check the Tiffany Falls visitor information for parking restrictions, fees and notices before setting out. For more trail planning, read our guide to hiking the Bruce Trail with your dog.

Hamilton Dog-Park Rules to Know Before You Go

The City’s dog-park rules require owners to pick up waste, keep dogs under voice control and leash them until inside the designated area.

  • One person may bring no more than two dogs at a time.
  • Dogs need valid rabies and dog-licence tags; an adult must accompany them.
  • Leave dog toys, food, rawhide chews and glass containers outside.
  • Don’t bring a sick dog, a dog in heat or an aggressive dog. Leave if your dog becomes aggressive.

Bring water and bags, monitor your dog and follow the signs at the entrance. For the separate backyard and public-space pickup duties, read our Hamilton dog poop bylaw guide.

Questions About Hamilton Dog Parks

Which Dundas dog park has a small-dog area?

The City’s published dog-park map marks a small-dog area at Borer’s Falls, 491 York Road at Valley Road. Check the enclosure signs when you arrive.

Is the Ancaster dog park fully fenced?

The City’s published map lists Corporal Nathan Cirillo Free Run Area at 799 Golf Links Road as unfenced. Check the live City finder and on-site boundaries before letting your dog off leash.

Can dogs run off leash in Dundas Valley?

No. Hamilton Conservation Authority requires dogs to remain leashed throughout Dundas Valley Conservation Area and its trails.
